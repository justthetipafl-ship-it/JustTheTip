This tool does not tell you what to bet. It shows you what the data says about a market — a player’s record, the matchup, and what the bookmakers are charging — so you can make your own call. Everything below explains how to read it.

The numbers on a row

Every suggestion shows the same handful of figures. These two matter most.

Model %

How often we think the bet lands. 73% model means 73 times in 100. It comes from the player’s own record, the matchup, and the sport’s model — then it is capped at what he has actually done, so a model that gets carried away is pulled back to his real form.

Edge %

What that estimate is worth at the price. It is not “22% more likely”. It is expected profit per dollar.

Under 0.5 Runs at $1.67 with Bet365
The price implies 1 ÷ 1.67 = 60% — that is the break-even rate
The model says 73%
0.73 × $1.67 = $1.22 back per $1 staked → +22% edge

So a positive edge means the model thinks the book is too short. It says nothing about any one bet: at 73%, losing happens more than once in four.

The rest

ScoreHow far above or below his usual level this projection sits. Useful for comparing two players, not for judging a price.
DVPDefence versus position: how much this opponent gives up in that stat compared with the league. +11% DVP is a soft matchup, a negative is a hard one.
Fair priceThe market’s own price with the bookmaker’s margin removed — what the bet would cost in a fair market.
x avg · n gmHis average in that stat, and how many games it is based on. A small sample is a warning, not a trend.
Orange chipsThings working against the bet. They are shown even when the overall read is positive.

How a signal is built

A signal is not “the model likes him”. Every tile has a rule, and a row only appears when the rule is met and the price still pays. That is why a tile is often empty: a strong read at a short price is filtered out rather than listed.

Two universal ones set the pattern:

  • Green Lights — the model and the price both say over, and the price pays for it.
  • Death Riders — the model and the price both say under.

A player appears once per tile, showing his strongest market, so one player the model likes cannot fill the whole tile.

The tools

Deep Dive (the telescope)

Everything behind one bet: the model’s read, his last 20 games against that line, his record cut seven ways, what actually drives that market, both teams’ numbers, the game script, his role and availability, and every bookmaker’s price.

Check My Bet

Type a bet in plain words — Daicos 30+ disposals — and it checks it the same way. Check My Multi does the same for a whole ticket, including how correlated the legs are.

Multi Builder

Builds a multi for one game. Filter by market, bookmaker and signal; nudge every line safer or riskier; every leg is priced at one bookmaker so the ticket can actually be placed.

My Card and the live tracker

The + on any row adds it to your card. During the game the tracker shows each leg’s progress live. A market the feed cannot see says not tracked rather than pretending.

Degenerates

Four ready-made builds: The Train (one banker per game), The Pyramid (one stat on falling lines), The Yankee (four same-game multis as an 11-bet system), and The Ladder (a running $10 challenge).

Common traps

One bet proves nothing. At 70%, three losses in a row is ordinary. The edge only shows up over hundreds of bets.

A big edge deserves suspicion. If the model is far away from the market, ask why the bookmaker is that short. Sometimes they know something we do not — a lineup change, an injury.

Check the sample. 5 gm next to a number means five games. That is not a trend.

Check the price is still there. Prices move. What we pulled may not be what you get.

A multi is not five good bets. Every leg multiplies the chance of losing, and legs from the same game move together.
